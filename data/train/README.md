# Training data

One JSON object per line in `*.jsonl` files:

```json
{"text": "Wat steiht morgen an?", "intent": "appointments_tomorrow", "timeframe": "tomorrow", "entity": null}
```

`timeframe` and `entity` are optional and default to `null`.

The holdout sets (`data/holdout_benchmarks.json`, blind v1, blind v2 and the
shadow set blind v3) are never used for training.
`tools/check_holdout_leak.py` rejects any training sentence that is identical
or close to a holdout sentence (character or word similarity of 0.85 or more);
the Data check workflow runs it on every change below `data/`.

`vyvy_train_v1.jsonl` is generated by `tools/build_train_data.py` from core
sentences written for it, in standard German and regional variants, with
fillers, lowercase and typos added. Persons and companies in it are invented.
The generator drops every sentence with a similarity of 0.8 or more to a
holdout sentence, which is stricter than the lock.

`unknown_negatives.txt` holds more than 300 sentences written for it that Vyvy
has no intent for: office and IT trouble, actions it cannot take (delete, move,
send, pay), questions about other people and companies, specialist knowledge,
arithmetic, small talk, everyday questions, writing help and regional speech.
The generator adds each of them once as `unknown`, without generated variants.

`vyvy_unknown_hard_v1.jsonl` holds 528 hard negatives for `unknown`, generated
by `tools/build_unknown_hard.py` from building blocks written for it: office
and everyday phrases (Mahlzeit, Bis morgen), incomplete or ambiguous fragments
(das Dokument von gestern, später vielleicht), private requests without a task
or calendar (weather, recipes, leisure) and other people's or companies'
calendars and mailboxes. About half carry a time word such as heute or
morgen, because the adapters of 08.11. read such words as a calendar request.
The last 48 lines (group 5) are dialect questions about weather, traffic,
trains, sport, food and evenings out, each with a time word and mostly a place
or a name (übamorgn z'Regensburg). They were written after the candidate of
09.11. failed one blind v2 question of that kind; they follow its pattern, not
its sentence, and come last so the lines before them stay unchanged.
Persons and companies are invented. The generator drops near copies of holdout
sentences (0.8), paraphrases of holdout sentences found by the semantic check
(listed in the generator), sentences already in the training data and sentences
close to a training sentence on the other side of the `unknown` boundary (0.85).

`vyvy_person_calendar_v1.jsonl` holds the 80 contrast pairs from the same
generator: read questions about the user's own calendar that name the same
persons (`appointments_today`, `appointments_tomorrow`, `daily_agenda`), with
`entity` as written in the sentence and the matching `timeframe`. "Ist Herr
Sommerfeld morgen im Büro?" is `unknown`, "Habe ich morgen was mit Herrn Sommerfeld?" is
`appointments_tomorrow`; without these the model could learn that a person plus
a time word means `unknown`. `--check` compares both committed files with the
generator; `tests/test_unknown_hard.py` runs it.

`vyvy_synth_<id>.jsonl` comes from a teacher model: `training/synthesize.py`
runs Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (Apache License 2.0) and Phi-4 (MIT) on a Kaggle GPU.
Qwen writes sentences per intent, speaking style and job; Phi-4, a different
model family, writes the hard cases (near misses between intents that are easy
to confuse, and "unknown" requests). Qwen labels every sentence again in a blind
second pass, and only sentences on which both agree are kept. The teacher never sees a holdout sentence;
`tools/import_synth.py` drops near copies of holdout sentences (0.8),
duplicates and, with the sentence model of the semantic check, every sentence
whose meaning is at 0.85 or more to a holdout sentence, before the file is
committed. From the round of 14.11. on, each sentence also carries its
`stratum`: 1 clean standard German, 2 everyday office German, 3 dialect, 4 hard
cases (near misses and `unknown`). `<id>` is the start of the script's
SHA-256, and `vyvy_synth_<id>.json` records teacher, counts per stratum and drops.

`vyvy_partner_<id>.jsonl` holds partner data: synthetic, anonymised sentences
handed over for Vyvy's intents, checked on import by `tools/import_partner.py`
(no e-mail address, phone number, IBAN, web address or street with house number;
output contract; holdout lock at 0.8; no duplicates). `<id>` is the start of the
handed-over file's SHA-256; `vyvy_partner_<id>.json` records what was dropped and
why. The Data check re-runs the personal-data check on every change.

No external dataset is used for training or loaded by a workflow; all data is
written for this repository or generated by an Apache-2.0 model from prompts
written for it.
